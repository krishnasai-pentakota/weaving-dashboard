# Findings

Kept in four bands. An *observation* is something the files show; an *analytical finding* is something computed from them; an *indicator* points somewhere without proving it; a *hypothesis* is untested.

## A. Direct observations
1. **The workbook labelled January is December** — its TOTAL tab runs 29-11-2025 to 31-12-2025.
2. **Units change mid-year** — December–March in metres, April onward in yards; quoted as yards without conversion, the metre months understate production by 8.6 %.
3. **The basis changes on 17 April** — rejection is deducted inside the total from that date.
4. **A formula fault zeroes real production** — confirmed in February (8 machine-days), March (8), May (20), June (31), July (6), August (5).
5. **Rejection is logged on days with no production column at all** — 8,764 yards on three August holidays.
6. **July's Summary tab cannot be reproduced from its own daily tab** — 2 of 30 days.
7. **The stop-reason box on the paper form is blank on about 98 % of rows** — the form had the box all along; the habit did not exist.
8. **One meter per loom** — 9.6 % of Jan–Aug yards were measured; 90.4 % are that one line multiplied.
9. **Loom counts contradict peer groups** on MA-26, MB-06 and MB-29.
10. **QC and production disagree** on specification for 3,401 events (167,870 yards).

## B. Analytical findings
1. **Plant efficiency Jan–Aug is 61.99 %** after removing target from stopped looms and filling 463 rows that had production but no target.
2. **18 % target problem, 82 % availability problem** — 5.31 M yards of shortfall sit above anything the loom has ever achieved; 23.48 M yards are the loom not delivering what it has delivered before.
3. **No target is out of reach** — 22 specifications realistic, 16 stretched, zero unsupported.
4. **August's loss is dominated by looms that ran below rate** — 2,851,481 of 4,380,182 lost yards (65 %); September 1–17 is 73 %.
5. **Availability is improving, rate is not** — June → September: availability 74.7 → 90.5 %, rate 64.8 → 68.8 %, quality flat at ~98.9 %.
6. **The shifts are level** — August's plant shift gap is 1.2 points; 43 looms have their own spread, an individual-loom matter.
7. **Loom diagnosis splits the shortfall by cause** — 17 looms pure availability, 6 pure rate, 24 both, 21 keeping up.
8. **Persistent underperformers** (bottom-ten weeks / weeks seen): MA-28 15/18, MA-22 20/26, MA-18 17/24, MA-24 13/20, MA-30 16/25, MA-02 19/30 — **four of the six are S-2 looms**.
9. **Recoverable production, August:** 760,112 yards if every loom reached its own peer group's rate; 163,002 from half the standing-still shifts; 32,140 if rejection fell to the best-quartile loom rate.
10. **Changeovers cost more than the day itself** — across 83 dated events, production is 121,989 yards below normal on the event day and 354,927 below on the day before; two-day cost 476,916 yards, about 5,746 per event.
11. **Quality is not the problem** — rejection is 1.1 % of what was woven.
12. **The audit moved production, not opinions** — roughly +145,000 yards restored and 77,415 removed, each written to an audit-log row.

## C. Investigative indicators
1. **289 shift rows / 217 machine-days book zero production while QC rejected cloth from that loom** — cloth cannot come from a loom that made nothing, so the metered line stopped while others kept weaving.
2. **The QC-vs-production specification conflict is patterned, not random** — QC writes 18800-035 where production says 18800-0034 on 1,882 rows.
3. **Supervisor rejection rates differ (4.60 % vs 3.02 %)** but the supervisor is missing on 73 % of August yards, so the ranking is indicative only.
4. **Span of control:** efficiency falls as looms-per-operator rises, but the fit explains only about 4 % of the variation (R² ≈ 0.04). Two more operators per shift projects roughly +2.4 points — presented as the size of the prize, not a promise.

## D. Hypotheses (untested)
1. The "ran below rate" loss is mostly short stops — warp and weft breaks — invisible in a daily odometer.
2. Some of it is lines fitted but not running.
3. Part of it is the target being set for a loom speed the loom no longer holds.

## Things this project deliberately does *not* claim
- No fraud score, fraud classification or misconduct conclusion — the methodology is data-forensic reconciliation and operational audit.
- No downtime figure — the existing record cannot measure it.
- No historical effort hours — no reliable time log exists.
